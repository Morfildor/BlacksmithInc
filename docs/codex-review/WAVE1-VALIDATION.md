## 3. Five most important remaining UX friction points

1. **F1 — Comparison still costs scroll-and-recall.** Keeping the tray open is a material improvement. However, the focused rules and delta follow available tiles, out-of-stock tiles and global matchup prose. In the ordinary Classic change, one additional swipe exposed the delta after selecting Silver. At 2.0 text, comparison captures show its heading near the foot while options have disappeared above. Experimentation is free mechanically, but remains expensive to read.
2. **F2 — Purpose is weaker at the result than before the craft.** Torvald's quality-34 failure was initially hidden under weapon stats, discoveries and rarity disclosure, while **List at 100** stayed prominent. The Shop later gave a clearer failure explanation. Wants have no equivalent named result assessment at all by source. This makes players reconstruct their original question exactly when they should learn whether the craft answered it.
3. **F3 — Party continuity works, but inspection remains administrative.** From a visible offer, opening, adding two members, reordering once, inspecting/backing out and confirming costs **seven taps plus scrolling**. The selected roster repeats its names in confirmation; long names make that footer a second scroller. Weapon-name-only summaries cannot distinguish the two Flaming Bronze Swords without opening equipment. This is friction, not a reason to add a roster dashboard.
4. **F4 — Readiness sometimes names someone the player cannot directly equip.** In ordinary Guild mornings, Hedda/Verity/Norrik were fallback town defenders, whereas direct loans apply to guild members. “Review their equipment or who stands” has a valid second route, but does not explain that control boundary. “3 of 3 defenders” also means filled places, not three armed, fit, guaranteed defenders. Gundra's warning correctly disappeared after her loan; the remaining warning was legitimate.
5. **F5 — A truthful contribution is not yet a lesson about the loss.** Swift's 9/92 is honest and traceable, but the headline does not explain the gravecaller's healing, the retreat or the fatal consequence. The detailed report and replay contain more evidence, behind additional reading. Hypothesis: players may remember a minor affix rather than an actionable preparation lesson. **Do not infer this from automation or change the standout policy without player evidence.**

The real strategic choices now include spending a scarce material versus retaining it, accepting rolled quality, replacing/loaning equipment, sending a party versus defending, and doing nothing. Those choices coexist with routine shelf, equipment and repeated-text administration. The two ordinary runs establish that choices can be performed and consequences found; they do not establish that the balance of choice to administration feels enjoyable.

## 7. Human-playtest checklist — five tasks

Use the roadmap's **6–8-player formative pass**, including enlarged-text users. Record completion, taps, scrolling/backtracking, explanation accuracy and unprompted reactions. This is not a retention study.

1. **Classic, serve a named need:** find a real want/commission, check existing stock, compare two recipes and decide whether a craft is needed. Explain purchase uncertainty and why the resulting weapon fits or fails. Do not teach direct hero assignment.
2. **Make and abandon an experiment:** change an ingredient, explain its known cost/effect tradeoff, put the original back, then leave Forge without crafting. Check that free experimentation and “Forge freely” are understood.
3. **Guild, plan with a sacrifice:** compare two contracts near a siege, state the return morning and who would miss the siege, then deliberately keep members home. Check whether optional work feels optional rather than unfinished.
4. **Guild, preserve intent:** select three heroes with long names, reorder them, loan/change a weapon through details, return and resolve revalidation, then submit or cancel. At font 1.5/2.0, verify the player can find both warning and confirmation and distinguish same-named weapons.
5. **Explain a siege and choose one next action:** read a costly hold/breach, identify actual forge damage versus current health and death versus being downed, find one weapon's recorded use, and explain what the evidence does **and does not** establish about the outcome. Find the returned blade or injured survivor next morning without prompting.

Provisional exit criterion from the roadmap: at least three quarters independently identify a purpose, one recipe tradeoff, siege availability and one recorded contribution. Collect separate qualitative evidence about tension, attachment and administrative fatigue; successful task automation cannot answer those questions.
